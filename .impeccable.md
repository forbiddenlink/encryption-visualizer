## Design Context

### Users
CryptoViz serves computer-science students, curious beginners, and developers who want to understand cryptographic transformations by experimenting, inspecting intermediate state, and checking their knowledge. The user delegated audience and direction decisions during this session.

### Brand Personality
Precise, curious, considered. Teach through visible cause and effect; make difficult mathematics approachable without making the subject childish.

### Aesthetic Direction
An illustrated scientific atlas: warm paper, deep ink, restrained vermilion accents, expressive Newsreader headings and clear DM Sans body text. Diagrams should recall carefully drawn scientific plates while remaining interactive. Both light and dark themes are fully supported. The user explicitly authorized overriding the prior instruction to route visual design to Claude on 2026-10-09.

### Design Principles
- Draw the operation, not a generic icon: matrices, curves, flows, schedules, and exchanges must correspond to the lesson.
- Establish a composed hierarchy with generous chapter spacing and tight functional groupings. Avoid nested cards and identical card grids.
- Use motion to explain transformations or feedback, with reduced-motion equivalents.
- Preserve readable technical data, native keyboard controls, visible focus, accessible contrast, and complete functionality on small screens.
- Preserve calculation correctness, assessment integrity, and existing progress while improving presentation across all routes.
